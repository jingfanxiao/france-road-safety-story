# Demo video script (about 3 minutes)

**Target length:** about 3:15 (limit 3:30). 394 spoken words ≈ 3:02 at 130 words per minute,
plus short pauses for the clicks.

All figures below are the ones the app shows for the default selection
(years 2019–2024, all of France). If the data is rebuilt, check them on screen before recording.

---

## Before recording: checklist

- [ ] Open the deployed app (Streamlit Community Cloud URL in the README) and wait until it has
      fully loaded. Visit each of the 4 pages once, so the cache is warm and pages open fast.
- [ ] Go back to **Story & Overview**. In the sidebar, click **Reset filters**, or check that the
      active selection reads "Years 2019–2024 · All of France · Indicator: Injury accidents".
- [ ] Browser zoom **80–90%**, so the KPI row and the trend chart fit on one screen. Full-screen
      window, 1920×1080 if possible.
- [ ] Keep the sidebar open. Close the *Context* expander and the *What if…* expander.
- [ ] Use the light theme for better contrast on video.
- [ ] Close other tabs, the bookmarks bar and chat apps. Turn on "Do not disturb" (Windows: Focus).
- [ ] Test the microphone: record one sentence and play it back.
- [ ] Keep this script on a second screen or on paper.

## Script

### 1 · Hook — 0:00–0:22 (Story & Overview)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Top of the *Story & Overview* page. Slowly move the mouse under the title and the hook paragraph (bold numbers). |
| **Say** | "Hello, I'm Jingfan Xiao. From 2019 to 2024, French police recorded more than 327,000 injury road accidents. Almost 20,000 people died, about 63 every week. But an accident is not equally dangerous everywhere. My app asks: where, and for whom, is an accident most likely to be fatal?" |
| **Reading time** | 48 words ≈ 22 s |

### 2 · Context and KPIs — 0:22–0:47 (Story & Overview)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Open the *Context: audience, key metric and data* expander, pause 2 s, close it. Point at the KPI row. In the sidebar, drag the **Years** slider to **2023–2024**; pause 2 s on the KPIs (accidents now 109,224). Click **Reset filters**. |
| **Say** | "The app is for local road-safety teams and citizens. The data is the official BAAC file from data.gouv.fr. The key metric is lethality: deaths per 100 injury accidents. If I move the year slider to 2023–2024, every KPI changes. Reset filters brings it back." |
| **Reading time** | 44 words ≈ 20 s (+ about 5 s of clicks) |

### 3 · Trend and 2020 — 0:47–1:07 (Story & Overview)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Point at the *Key insight* box under "Are French roads getting safer?". Scroll to the trend chart. Hover over the 2020 point (shaded area) on both lines. |
| **Say** | "This key insight is computed from the data. Accidents fell by 7.5%, but deaths only by 1.9%. So lethality went up, from 5.9 to 6.3. In 2020, the lockdown year, both lines drop to about 80% of 2019." |
| **Reading time** | 38 words ≈ 18 s |

### 4 · Where: the map — 1:07–1:40 (Where)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Click **Where** in the navigation. Point at the *Key insight* box. The map shows *Accidents per year*: hover over **Paris** (small dark area in the north). Click the radio button **Lethality**; pause 2 s. Hover over **Jura** (east, on the Swiss border). |
| **Say** | "Now, where. Do the départements with the most accidents have the deadliest ones? No, it is the opposite. On accident volume, big cities are darkest. Paris has only 0.8 deaths per 100 accidents. Now I switch to lethality. The map flips: rural départements become dark. Jura has 17.4. In the quietest quarter, an accident is 3.3 times as lethal as in the busiest quarter." |
| **Reading time** | 64 words ≈ 30 s |

### 5 · Where: top 10 and what-if — 1:40–2:07 (Where)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Scroll to *Top 10 départements by lethality*. Hover over the grey bar (**Wallis et Futuna**). Scroll down and open the *What if…* expander. The slider starts at 10; drag it to **20**, pause, then back to **10**. |
| **Say** | "This chart ranks the ten most lethal départements. The grey bar has fewer than 100 accidents, so its rate is not reliable. Now a simple what-if. If the 10 deadliest départements had the national lethality, about 154 deaths a year would be avoided. With 20, about 291. This is only a calculation, not a prediction." |
| **Reading time** | 54 words ≈ 25 s |

### 6 · Who and when — 2:07–2:40 (Who)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Click **Who**. Hover over the **Pedestrian** bar, then the **Car** bar. Scroll to the heatmap (it shows *Injury accidents*): point at the dark cells around 17h, Monday to Friday. In the sidebar, change **Indicator** to **Lethality**; pause 2 s; point at the dark cells at night. |
| **Say** | "Next, who. Pedestrians are the most exposed: about 5 in 100 involved died. That is 2.3 times the rate in a car. Vulnerable users, like pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists, make 46% of all deaths. The heatmap shows accidents peak on weekdays around 5 pm. Now I change the indicator to lethality. The dark cells move to the night. The most lethal hour is 5 am." |
| **Reading time** | 65 words ≈ 30 s |

### 7 · Data quality, conclusions, limits — 2:40–3:12 (Data Quality & Conclusions)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Click **Data Quality & Conclusions**. Point at the *Key insight* box. Scroll slowly through the checks table (coloured status) and the missing-values chart. Scroll to **Conclusions**, then **Limitations** and **Next steps**. |
| **Say** | "Last page: can we trust the data? I checked duplicates, missing values, coordinates and the links between tables. 17 of 26 checks pass, and the 3 warnings are explained. Three conclusions: look at lethality, not only counts. Rank territories by lethality too. And protect people outside a car. Limits: only police-recorded accidents, and no traffic data. So these are associations, not causes. Next step: add traffic data." |
| **Reading time** | 67 words ≈ 31 s |

### 8 · Closing — 3:12–3:20

| | |
|---|---|
| **Screen** | Scroll back to the top of the page. Keep the mouse still. |
| **Say** | "To sum up: the busiest places are not the deadliest. Thank you for watching." |
| **Reading time** | 14 words ≈ 6 s |

## Recording tips

- **Move the mouse slowly**, and keep it still while you talk about a chart.
- **Pause 1–2 seconds after every click** (page change, slider, radio button, indicator) so the
  page can redraw before you talk about it.
- **Hold each tooltip** for about 1 second so viewers can read it.
- **Record in parts**, for example one take per page, then join them in a video editor. It is
  easier to redo one part than the whole video.
- **Speak slowly.** Short pauses at full stops are fine. A bit slow is better than too fast.
- **Pronunciation:** "départements" ≈ *day-part-MON*; "lethality" ≈ *lee-THAL-i-tee*;
  "BAAC" ≈ *B-A-A-C* letter by letter.
- **Check the numbers on screen** before each take. If you changed a filter by mistake, click
  *Reset filters*.
- Export in 1080p MP4 and check the sound level on the final file.
